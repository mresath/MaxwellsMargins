# Math and Physics Notes

## Electrostatics

Each point charge $q_i$ at position $\vec r_i$ contributes a field and potential at point
$\vec r$:

$$
\vec E_i(\vec r) = k\frac{q_i}{|\vec r - \vec r_i|^2}\hat u_i, \qquad
V_i(\vec r) = k\frac{q_i}{|\vec r - \vec r_i|}
$$

where $\hat u_i$ is the unit vector from the charge to $\vec r$ and $k$ is Coulomb's
constant (`COULOMB_CONSTANT` in `Config.hpp`). By superposition:

$$
\vec E(\vec r) = \sum_i \vec E_i(\vec r), \qquad V(\vec r) = \sum_i V_i(\vec r)
$$

Force between two charges (Coulomb's law):

$$
F = k\frac{|q_1 q_2|}{r^2}
$$

**Field lines** are traced by stepping a small distance along $\vec E$ from seed points
around each charge until leaving the canvas or approaching another charge.

**Equipotential contours** are extracted from the potential grid at a chosen value of $V$
(marching-squares style, see `EquipotentialTracer`).

**Gauss's law**: for a user-drawn closed surface (circle, `GaussianSurface`), the flux
through it equals the enclosed charge over the permittivity of free space:

$$
\Phi = \oint \vec E \cdot d\vec A = \frac{Q_{enclosed}}{\varepsilon_0}
$$

`GaussianSurface::enclosedCharge` sums the charges whose position is inside `radius` of
`center`; `flux` follows directly from the enclosed charge, independent of the charges'
exact positions (as guaranteed by Gauss's law) or of any charges outside the surface.

## Magnetism

**Uniform field**: `UniformBField::strength` is signed - positive means out of the page,
negative means into the page.

**Lorentz force** on a moving charge:

$$
\vec F = q\vec E + q\vec v \times \vec B
$$

For a charge in a uniform $B$ field with no $E$ field, this produces uniform circular
motion (or a helix, in 3D) with radius:

$$
r = \frac{mv}{|q|B}
$$

**Current-carrying wires**: the field around a straight wire segment follows
Biot-Savart/Ampere (right-hand rule):

$$
B = \frac{\mu_0 I}{2\pi d}
$$

at perpendicular distance $d$ from the wire, circling the wire per the right-hand rule.

**Force between two parallel current-carrying wires** (attractive if currents are parallel,
repulsive if antiparallel):

$$
\frac{F}{L} = \frac{\mu_0 I_1 I_2}{2\pi d}
$$

**Force on a current-carrying segment** in an external field:

$$
\vec F = I\vec L \times \vec B
$$

## Induction

**Faraday's law**: the EMF induced in a loop equals the negative rate of change of
magnetic flux through it:

$$
\mathcal{E} = -\frac{d\Phi_B}{dt}, \qquad \Phi_B = \vec B \cdot \vec A = BA\cos\theta
$$

where $\theta$ is the angle between the field and the loop's area normal. `MovingLoop`
tracks `lastFlux` each step so `inducedEMF` can be computed from the change in flux over
`dt`.

**Lenz's law**: the induced current's direction opposes the change in flux that produced
it (the minus sign above) - visualized as a direction indicator on the loop.

**Generator demo**: a loop of $N$ turns, area $A$, rotating at constant angular velocity
$\omega$ in a uniform field $B$ produces a sinusoidal EMF:

$$
\mathcal{E}(t) = NBA\omega\sin(\omega t)
$$

## Circuits

Solved each step via **modified nodal analysis (MNA)**: unknowns are node voltages (and
branch currents for voltage sources/inductors); equations come from Kirchhoff's current
law (KCL) at each node plus the constitutive relation of each component
($V=IR$ for resistors, $I = C\,dV/dt$ for capacitors, $V = L\,dI/dt$ for inductors, fixed
EMF for batteries). This generalizes to arbitrary topologies without needing to detect
independent loops, unlike mesh analysis.

**RC transient** (charging, switch closes at $t=0$ with initial charge 0):

$$
V_C(t) = V_0\left(1 - e^{-t/RC}\right), \qquad I(t) = \frac{V_0}{R}e^{-t/RC}
$$

**RL transient** (current rising toward $V_0/R$):

$$
I(t) = \frac{V_0}{R}\left(1 - e^{-tR/L}\right)
$$

These transients are integrated over time by the shared solver rather than solved
analytically, so arbitrary combinations (switches opening mid-transient, series/parallel
networks) work without special-casing.

## Solver

A single fixed-step Dormand-Prince-style (DOPRI5) integrator (`engine/Solver`) advances
state vectors for: charged-particle motion (position/velocity), RC/RL transient state
(capacitor charge / inductor current), and the rotating-loop generator (angle/angular
velocity). Using one solver everywhere (vs. Newton's Notepad's 8-solver comparison shelf)
keeps behavior consistent across domains, per the "single robust solver" requirement.
